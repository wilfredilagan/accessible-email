Accessible email updates

Exciting changes are coming to your inbox! Hilton’s emails are changing and, in the process, becoming more accessible. The two biggest changes impacting the accessibility of emails are:

  • the move towards “live text” content
  • new projects aimed at creating libraries of email components that will make building new emails more efficient, consistent, and accessible.

Live text emails, that is emails that utilize programmatic text instead of rendering everything in an email as part of an image, have been rolling out throughout 2025. Compared with previous email designs that had all content contained within images, live text emails are a significantly more accessible experience in three key ways.

  1. Live text emails are easier to read for screen reader users. These users can now identify headings, jump through content, and listen to only the portions of emails that they’re interested in.
  2. People who use high contrast mode or increase their default font size benefit from live text. With live text users’ settings are respected and content is easier to read (example in image for article).
  3. The third major benefit is for users who don’t load images for any reason. Live text emails ensure readable content is present for everyone.

In addition to the transition to live text emails a new effort is beginning to build libraries of components that can be quickly assembled into new emails. Like our component libraries for the web, these will be built to be accessible out of the box. Once complete these libraries will make consistent accessibility a core part of Hilton’s email development.

Evolving perspectives: disability across generations

This year, the Americans with Disabilities Act turned 35, while the European Accessibility Act has just become mandatory for businesses. These two milestones mark decades of progress, but also decades of evolving perspectives on disability across generations.

And speaking of generations, September is Intergenerational Month and it’s a time to reflect on the values, experiences, and shifts that have shaped each generation with our Generations TMRG. It’s the perfect moment to look at how attitudes toward disability have transformed over time.

Baby Boomers (1946–1964) were born into a society where disability was stigmatized and rarely discussed. Over time, they saw major legislative changes—like the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 and the ADA (1990)—that began removing systemic barriers. As Boomers aged, disability rates rose due to chronic conditions such as arthritis and diabetes.

Gen X (1965–1980) grew up during the rollout of these laws. They experienced higher disability rates earlier than Boomers, often linked to mental health and obesity-related conditions. Their careers aligned with the rise of digital accessibility standards like Section 508 and Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG).

Millennials (1981–1996) were raised with inclusive education and emerging assistive tech. They saw increased diagnoses of ADHD, anxiety, and depression, helping expand the definition of disability and normalize mental health conversations.

Gen Z (1997–2012) grew up with disability as part of mainstream discourse. They report higher rates of neurodivergence and use social media to advocate for equity and systemic change.

Gen Alpha (2013–2025) is growing up with universal design, early screening, and AI-powered assistive tools, setting a new baseline for accessibility from early childhood.

So, as we celebrate Intergenerational Month, let’s also recognize how far we’ve come in our understanding of disability. How each generation has played a role in moving us from stigma to empowerment, from silence to advocacy, and from exclusion to equity. Let us know how your views on disability have changed through the years by commenting on the SharePoint Version of this article and for more information about the Generations TMRG, go to their page on the TMRG Engagement Hub.

Accessibility office hours now on Accessibility Central

Starting in the middle of September, the accessibility team will now be holding its Accessibility Office Hours on the Accessibility Central Teams Channel. The time will stay the same, Thursdays from 2:00pm – 3:00pm Central Standard Time, but you can now find an invite on the General channel of Accessibility Central. Anyone can add the invite to their calendar and the invite is also sharable. We will also be announcing the change in team meetings as well as in office hours before launch.

Accessibility Central is the accessibility team’s channel for all things accessibility. In the channel, you can ask any of your burning accessibility questions and we’ll also be sharing out some info/tips regarding a range of topics surrounding accessibility. By having questions on the channel, the team hopes that this will allow for some flexibility in our support, so we can help team members who have accessibility questions but are unable to attend the Office Hours event itself.

Watch for more updates in the Accessibility Office Hours and in the Accessibility Central Team.
