Making Accessibility Everyone’s Business: Hilton’s Inclusive Approach to QE

In the ever-evolving world of digital experiences, accessibility isn’t just a checklist, it’s a commitment. At Hilton, the Quality Engineering (QE) team has taken this commitment to heart, embedding accessibility into every step of their development workflow. Thanks to the close collaboration with our accessibility team, Hilton has transformed its testing processes, culture, and ultimately, the guest experience across all 24 brands.

Accessibility testing at Hilton is not an afterthought. It’s seamlessly integrated into the QE workflow, with every ticket undergoing thorough accessibility checks right after functional testing. The scope spans from screen reader support, using JAWS for web, TalkBack for Android, and VoiceOver for iOS, to core usability features like keyboard navigation, zoom functionality, and semantic headings. It’s a bold statement: accessibility is no longer an add-on; it’s standard.

This shift didn’t happen overnight. According to a QE engineer, Rachana Dixit, accessibility testing began back in 2014 with limited awareness and rudimentary checks. But over time, the teams cultivated deeper expertise, developed rigorous testing protocols, and streamlined priorities. Where there was once a massive backlog of accessibility defects, issues are now treated with the same urgency and importance as any other bugs. Automation tools like Cypress have also stepped in, finding heading issues, unlabeled form fields and some minor focus issues.

A standout feature of Hilton’s approach is the embedded partnership with the accessibility team. Each product pod includes an accessibility analyst who’s involved from as early as the requirements phase, then through the UX design phase and onward. This proactive approach ensures accessibility is considered before QE even begins testing. And when questions arise, QE testers receive direct support from experts on the accessibility team creating a seamless feedback loop.

New QE team members are trained with accessibility in mind, reinforcing the belief that inclusive design is everyone’s responsibility. The results speak for themselves: Hilton has significantly reduced its backlog of accessibility defects and consistently delivers accessible digital experiences across web and mobile platforms.

Inclusion isn’t just a goal; it’s baked into the workflow. And with accessibility woven into Hilton’s engineering fabric, the future of travel looks more welcoming for everyone.

Dining with Hugh Contest Answers

  1. What would be the most reasonable accommodation to make the reservation process easier for Hugh (who has dyslexia)?
    Answer: Booking a table online and allowing for confirmation or edits via email or app if there is a mistake in the booking.
  2. What is one accommodation Hugh could ask for once he arrives and is given a menu?
    Answer: A digital version so that he can use his screen reader.
  3. Hugh's phone is dead. What would make the physical menu handed to Hugh more accessible? (Choose three)
    Answer:
    1. A picture alternative to text for the individual dishes
    2. Clear icons with legends to show food allergies
    3. Clear bold headings to distinguish between the categories of options.
  4. During his meal, Hugh has to go to the restroom. Choose 2 barriers related to his disability he could face trying to find his way there.
    Answer:
    1. There is no clear signage with images or icons that provide direction
    2. The signs on the bathroom doors do not use recognized symbols

Awards and Recognition

Red Dot Design Award: Hilton Connected Room Remote Control

We’re thrilled to announce that Hilton has been honored with the prestigious Red Dot Design Award 2025 for our Hilton Connected Room Remote Control, a shining example of innovation meeting inclusion. Developed in collaboration with design experts, the remote was created with a clear focus on accessibility and intuitive operation.

Featuring high contrast visuals, clear symbols, and enlarged fonts, the remote simplifies control of streaming, lighting, temperature, and TV functions. A distinct grooved metal ring centers navigation, while tactile markings support effortless, haptic orientation.

According to the Red Dot Jury: “With its exemplary clarity and intuitive usability, the Hilton Connected Room Remote Control significantly improves the guest experience.”

This award reflects our dedication to inclusive design and personalized technology, ensuring every guest feels at home in a Hilton stay.

Hilton Partners with AccessAble to Elevate Guest Accessibility

Hilton’s partnership with AccessAble, the UK and Ireland’s leading provider of detailed accessibility information, ensures that all travelers have all of the information they need when they stay at a Hilton hotel. With 60 Hilton properties across eight brands now featured, we have the largest number of reviewed sites of any hospitality group on the AccessAble platform.

From car parking to lighting levels, every detail has been carefully surveyed by trained accessibility experts, empowering guests with access needs to plan their stays confidently and comfortably. These guides are designed with usability in mind, offering facts, figures, and photos that speak to individual needs, not vague labels.

This collaboration aligns perfectly with our promise to put accessibility at the heart of hospitality. Whether it’s for work, celebration, or adventure, Hilton guests now have even more tools to tailor their experience.
